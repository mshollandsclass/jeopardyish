Build your board from lessons you already taught

Attach two things to Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini: the CSV template above, and your lesson materials. Worksheets, guided notes, slide decks, the reading students did, your study guide, unit plan, vocabulary list, past quizzes, lab handouts, or your standards document all work. Attach several — the more the assistant sees, the closer the questions land to what your students did.

Then paste this prompt and fill in the bracketed parts:

I've attached my Jeopardyish template and my lesson materials. Write review
questions drawn from my materials and give the filled template back to me as a
CSV file I can download.

MY CLASS
Subject: [your subject]
Grade level: [your grade]
Unit or topic: [your unit]

WHAT I'VE ATTACHED
[List what you attached, for example: three worksheets, my slide deck, and the
study guide. Delete the ones that don't apply.]

WHAT STUDENTS NEED TO KNOW BY THE END
[Paste your learning objectives, essential questions, or standards here. If you
don't have them written out, list the 5 to 10 things students must walk away
knowing.]

WHAT I ALREADY ASSESSED OR SKIPPED
[Optional. Note anything to leave out — a lesson you cut, a section you'll test
separately, or a topic students already mastered. Write "nothing" if this
doesn't apply.]

BUILD THE QUESTIONS FROM MY MATERIALS
- Pull every question from what I attached. Do not add outside content, and do
  not test anything my materials never covered.
- Use my exact vocabulary and terminology. If my worksheet says "producer," do
  not write "autotroph."
- Weight the board toward what my materials spend the most time on. If three
  worksheets cover photosynthesis and one covers soil, photosynthesis gets more
  questions.
- Build categories around the actual sections, worksheets, or lessons in my
  materials rather than generic topic names.
- If my materials don't contain enough content to fill all 61 questions, tell
  me which categories came up short before you write anything.

WHAT TO CHANGE IN THE TEMPLATE
- Replace every "Category 1" through "Category 6" with a real category name
- Replace every "Question 1" through "Question 5" with a real question
- Replace every "Answer 1" through "Answer 5" with the matching answer
- Replace "Final Jeopardish Question", "Final Question", and "Final Answer"
  with a real Final Round category, question, and answer

WHAT TO LEAVE ALONE
- Keep the header line exactly as it is: round,category,value,question,answer
- Do not change any number in the round column
- Do not change any number in the value column
- Do not add rows, remove rows, or reorder rows
- Keep all 62 lines, including the header

HOW TO WRITE THE QUESTIONS
- Category names: 30 characters maximum, 22 or fewer is better
- Round 1 and Round 2 need different categories from each other
- Write the question column as a statement, the way a game show clue reads
  Example: This organelle converts sunlight into sugar.
- Write the answer column as a question
  Example: What is a chloroplast?
- Difficulty rises with the value: in each category the lowest value is recall
  straight from my materials, and the highest value asks students to apply or
  connect ideas
- Round 2 should be harder than Round 1 throughout
- Make the Final Round question the hardest one — it should require connecting
  two or more ideas from across the unit
- Keep questions under 200 characters so they read clearly on a projector
- Write at a reading level appropriate for [your grade]
- Never repeat a question or reuse an answer
- Wrap any question or answer containing a comma in double quotes

BEFORE YOU GIVE ME THE FILE
Confirm the file still has 62 lines, the round and value columns are untouched,
and no placeholder text is left anywhere. Then list any question you were not
able to source directly from my materials.

Read every question against your materials before you upload. AI assistants write fluent clues and still get facts wrong. Treat the result as a first draft, not a finished board.

If something looks off:

  • Questions don't match what you taught — tell it to rewrite using only the attached materials, and to name the worksheet each question came from.
  • Wrong terminology — tell it to use your materials' exact vocabulary and give two or three examples it got wrong.
  • Fewer than 60 questions in the preview — ask it to return all 62 lines.
  • Placeholder text on the board — ask it to replace every remaining "Question" and "Answer" placeholder.
  • Round 2 cards look empty — it changed the value column. Ask it to restore 200, 400, 600, 800, 1000 for Round 2.
  • Categories cut off — a name ran past 30 characters. Ask for shorter names.
  • You got text instead of a file — ask it to save the result as a downloadable CSV.
